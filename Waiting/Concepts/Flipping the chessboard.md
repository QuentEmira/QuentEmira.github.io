A variant of [[Reversal]].

Introduced in [[Episode 1, what happened.|Umineko]].

### Concept
- If you take a situation as two people playing on a chessboard, flipping the board is to a certain extent telling to think from the other person's perspective. This often involve question why certain actions and decisions were made the way they were.
- This is generally used in the context of another person committing a crime, but has further use cases.
- It matches reversal, but is not the same, as it has an active purpose in its usage.
- This kind of thinking is weak against noise. In chess, as the rules don't change over time, a person 100 years from now might arrive at the same conclusion as someone from 100 years before, from a given position. But introduce noise, fluctuations, mistakes and incorrect knowledge, and see this method fall apart.
- There is no proper way to check if the facts we work on are true or false in this. We can reason out, but never truly assert something is right or wrong. This would mean we would be stuck in a situation where we go back and forth, seeing both sides, but being unable to reach a proper conclusion.
- Another problem, which is a continuation of the above, is that you need to understand your enemies goal to be able to predict the 'best move'. Even then, it is not necessary for them to play the best move. Also, if you have no idea or understanding of the enemy, or their motives, this method will only produce rubbish. Garbage in, Garbage out.. 
### In Game Usage
- This is a technique taught by Kyrie to Battler.
- Kyrie uses this to reason that Rudolph wants us to ask him questions and get the information out, even though he said he doesn't want to talk about it.
- It is her interpretation of Game Theory. Game theory itself comes down to math. So, in a way, this a mathematical way of thinking about something.
### Practice
- When we come to a conclusion, or a dead-end, flip the board and think from the opponents view. Is this what the opponent wants? Why do they want this? Why would they want this?
- Practice it actively.
