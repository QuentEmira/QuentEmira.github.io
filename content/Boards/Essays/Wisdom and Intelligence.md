Based on the Essay available in Paul Graham's website, '[Is it worth being wise?](https://paulgraham.com/wisdom.html)'

## Essay, in short
### Curve
In the essay, he talks about what could be the difference between wisdom and intelligence.
As far as I understand, the way to differentiate between wisdom and intelligence is based on a curve on a graph. If one plots the situations on the x axis, and the quality of the solution one comes up with on the y axis, then wisdom is when the plot where the quality is high across all situations, that the person knows what to do or what the best option is across multiple situations. Intelligence is more like a spike, where the quality of the solution is very high, even if it is specific to a situation.

It seems to fit with conventional wisdom of wisdom being related to human problems, whereas intelligence being associated with other problems.

### Split
- A person has to choose between both wisdom and intelligence.
- Wisdom and intelligence converge to become almost the same thing as you decrease the number of points (situations) that a person has to go through. But as the number of points increases, it is clear that there is a difference in the way wisdom and intelligence is assessed.
- So, one has to choose. Wisdom, or intelligence?
- During older times, wisdom and intelligence have been regarded as the same thing, because the number of situations have been less.
- But as time progressed, and humanity developed, people now have access to more situations, are put in different kinds of situations, and thus it is becoming clear that wisdom and intelligence are difference.
- Wisdom and intelligence are not mutually exclusive.
### New
- Human knowledge grows in fractals. As one discover more, they realise that there is a lot more to discover in the new fractal buds they have discovered.
- This lead to a lot more new things needing to be found to expand and harvest on this knowledge.
- But here comes the difference. Wisdom does not help with making things. A lot of things associated with wisdom lies in the domain of comparing and contrasting between the options given for the question. Intelligence would be essential to find new discoveries.
- Prudence can advice in what to tell to people. It doesn't help in deciding what sentence to write next in a story. The search space is too big.

### Limits
- When comparing and choosing the best option, there is an upper limit - the best options. One can be content knowing that the best options has been chosen.
- But when it comes to making or inventing, the sky is the limit. There is no proper thing to compare with. What one can do is compare with other people, or their own work, but that itself would be an imperfect metric. Thus, people who make things, or invent things, are often discontent.
- This is what quotes about wisdom say, that the 'small man is discontent with his life, while a wise man is not.'. The difference here is that the type of job that people do has evolved over time, and that this advice might be obsolete.

### Recipe
- The way to wisdom seems different than the way to intelligence.
- For wisdom, cultivating self-control and experience in order to control and curb random thoughts seems to be an important part of it. This would mean being more neutral, and not really indulging oneself into something severely.
- For intelligence, its different. It is actively solving hard problems and challenging yourself. For this, one needs genuine curiosity. One needs to be biased towards a somethings. One needs to indulge oneself in something.
- Thus, the recipe for wisdom and intelligence is different. This is also one reason why we might have to choose between them.

### Different
 - Discontent is a part of the intelligent process. One has to learn to accept it.
